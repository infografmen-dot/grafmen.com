A well-prepared brief is the foundation for the smooth implementation of a website. It allows you to precisely define the business goals, functional scope, and budget framework of the project.

---

## 1. What is the main goal of the new website?

Before we move on to colors and the layout of subpages, it is crucial to define the role of the website in the company:
- **Acquiring sales inquiries (lead generation):** The priority is clear forms and distinct calls to action (CTA).
- **Building brand image and credibility:** We focus on the portfolio, testimonials, and unique identity.
- **Presenting a product or service offer:** Clear information architecture and categorization are essential.

---

## 2. Who is your audience and what do they want to find?

The website shouldn't only appeal to the owner – above all, it must answer the questions and needs of potential clients:
1. What client problems does your service solve?
2. What objections do you most often encounter during sales conversations?
3. What materials and proof of quality (certificates, completed projects) convince them to choose your company?

---

## 3. What is worth preparing before contacting a designer?

To ensure the pricing and design process goes smoothly without delays, prepare:
- Logo in a vector format (SVG, PDF, AI, or EPS).
- A basic list of planned tabs and subpages.
- Examples of 2–3 websites whose aesthetics or functionality you like (with a brief explanation why).
- The estimated launch date for the website.
